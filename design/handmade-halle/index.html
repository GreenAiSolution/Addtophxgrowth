monogram · h g

The sketch already had the idea in it.

The h's shoulder doesn't stop where the h stops — it carries straight on and becomes the bowl of the g. That join is the mark. Everything else here exists to protect it: one continuous line, one pen weight, and a tail that earns its length by swinging under and crossing back through the descender.

origin

From the pencil

It was drawn on ruled notebook paper, which is where this whole page gets its colours — graphite, the printed rule, the margin line down the left. The vector keeps the sketch's proportions and its three decisions: a slim looped ascender, a bowl hung off the h's shoulder, and a tail that rises back across itself.

Kept: the h-to-g join, the narrow ascender loop, the tail crossing the descender at roughly two-thirds height. Trued: the bowl to an even oval, the pen to a single width, and the tail to a taper that thins across its last third instead of stopping flat.

the mark

Three ways it appears

The monogram is the identity. It carries the brand alone on anything small — a tag, an avatar, a stamped box — and the wordmark joins it only where there's room to set it properly.

lockups

Two lockups, and no more

The stacked lockup is the signature: shop front, packaging, anywhere the brand introduces itself. The horizontal one exists only for places a stacked mark can't go — a site header, an email footer, the top of an invoice.

palette

Palette

Six values, all of them off the page the mark was drawn on. Graphite sets every piece of type. The rule and the margin line are for hairlines only — neither has the contrast to carry text, and neither should be asked to.

in use

In use

The mark was made with a pencil and should keep looking like it was. Print it as a single flat colour — stamped, foiled, or pressed into stock with some tooth to it. It has no gradient, no fill and no shadow, and it never needs one.

spec

Clear space and minimum size

Clear space is the height of the ascender loop, measured off the mark itself so it scales with it. Nothing sets inside that — not a rule, not a caption, not the edge of a label.

files

Files

Every SVG draws in currentColor, so it takes the colour of whatever it's placed in. All of them regenerate from build.py — the curves live there once, rather than in six files that drift apart the first time one is nudged.
